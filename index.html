📚 Lesson Summary & Formulae

Key Concept: To convert a recurring decimal to a fraction, define it as $$x$$, multiply by appropriate powers of $$10$$ to align the repeating tails, and subtract to eliminate the infinite decimals.

Quick Rule: Single recurring digit $$\rightarrow$$ divide by $$9$$. Two recurring digits $$\rightarrow$$ divide by $$99$$. Mixed recurring decimals require aligning powers of $$10$$ before subtracting.

💡 Step-by-Step Worked Examples

Step 1: Let $$x = 0.777...$$

Step 2: Multiply by $$10$$: $$10x = 7.777...$$

Step 3: Subtract $$x$$: $$9x = 7 \implies x = \frac{7}{9}$$.

Analysis: Ted multiplied by $$10$$ incorrectly without aligning the recurring tails properly ($$0.4333...$$ vs $$4.3434...$$). Proper alignment requires multiplying by $$100$$ and $$10$$ for mixed recurring decimals.

Step 1: Let $$x = 0.25656...$$

Step 2: $$1000x = 256.5656...$$ and $$10x = 2.5656...$$

Step 3: Subtract to get $$990x = 254 \implies x = \frac{127}{495}$$.
